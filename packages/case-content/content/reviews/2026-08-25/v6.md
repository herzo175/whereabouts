# Whereabouts themed review: 2026-08-25

## Theme: Historic Walkable Bridges in Cities

Old spans carry footsteps over rivers, linking city streets to centuries of travel.

### Inclusion criteria

Include only a location satisfying every condition: (1) it is an individually named bridge or bridge complex whose established function is carrying people across water, a ravine, or another physical obstacle; (2) the present bridge retains substantial historic fabric and was opened, completed, or substantially rebuilt at its current site on or before 31 December 1899; (3) ordinary pedestrians can currently cross or walk onto it during normal public access, whether freely or by admission; (4) it lies within the current administrative boundary of a present-day city or incorporated urban municipality; (5) the bridge itself, rather than merely a viewpoint or surrounding district, can be pinned as one exact map location. Plausible locations include Charles Bridge (Prague), Rialto Bridge (Venice), Ponte Vecchio (Florence), Ponte Sant’Angelo (Rome), Ponte Sisto (Rome), Ponte Pietra (Verona), Ponte Scaligero (Verona), Ponte di Tiberio (Rimini), Ponte Coperto (Pavia), Pont Neuf (Paris), Pont Marie (Paris), Pont de la Tournelle (Paris), Pont Saint-Bénézet (Avignon), Pont Vieux (Albi), Pont Valentré (Cahors), Stari Most (Mostar), Krämerbrücke (Erfurt), Pulteney Bridge (Bath), Old Bridge (Heidelberg), Stone Bridge (Regensburg), Clifton Suspension Bridge (Bristol), Bridge of Sighs (Cambridge), Mathematical Bridge (Cambridge), Brooklyn Bridge (New York City), Eads Bridge (St. Louis), Széchenyi Chain Bridge (Budapest), Liberty Bridge (Budapest), Margaret Bridge (Budapest), Triple Bridge (Ljubljana), Stone Bridge (Skopje), Kintai Bridge (Iwakuni), Puente Romano (Córdoba), Puente Romano (Mérida), Puente de Piedra (Zaragoza), Puente de San Martín (Toledo), Puente de Alcántara (Toledo), Puente Nuevo (Ronda), Puente de Besalú, and Puente de la Reina Victoria (Madrid).
